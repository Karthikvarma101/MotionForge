Before you start

Install and open MotionForge

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 26 or later.

  1. Download MotionForge.dmg, open it and drag MotionForge onto the Applications folder.
  2. Open MotionForge from Applications. The first time, macOS says “Apple could not verify…” because this build isn't notarised by Apple yet. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to MotionForge. You only do this once.
  3. The app plays a short logo animation (click to skip it), then shows the Home window.

Home is where every project starts:

  • New Project (⌥⌘N) opens an empty 1920 × 1080, 30 fps, 10-second composition.
  • Open Project… (⌘O) opens a saved .mfproj file.
  • Starter Scene opens a small finished animation. Open it first and press Space to play: it's a good thing to poke at.
  • Recent projects appear as cards with thumbnails. Click one to open it.
Tip. You can come back to Home any time with the house button at the left of the toolbar, or ⇧⌘H. When a new version is out, Home tells you, and Update Now installs it.
The basics

Six words to know

Every motion-graphics app, MotionForge included, is built on the same few ideas. Learn these and the rest of the app makes sense.

Composition

Your canvas and its length in time: a size (say 1920 × 1080), a frame rate (say 30 frames per second) and a duration. A project can hold several compositions.

Layer

One thing in the composition: a shape, a piece of text, an image, a video clip, a sound, a camera. Layers stack: the one at the top of the timeline is drawn in front.

Property

A setting on a layer that you can change, such as Position, Scale, Rotation, Opacity or an effect's strength.

Keyframe

A saved value of a property at one moment. Set two keyframes with different values and MotionForge fills in every frame between them. That's animation.

Playhead

The blue line in the timeline marking the current time. Keyframes are written wherever the playhead is.

Easing

How a change speeds up and slows down between keyframes. Easing is the difference between movement that feels robotic and movement that feels real.

Find your way around

The workspace

Open the Starter Scene from Home. The editor has six areas:

  1. Toolbar. Home, buttons that add shapes, text and media, the tools (selection, hand, zoom, pen and more), the Viewer | Nodes switch, panel buttons, Claude, undo / redo, Share, Export and your account.
  2. Project panel. Your compositions and imported media, plus the Effects tab with every effect and preset. The Import… button sits at the bottom.
  3. Viewer. The picture. Click a layer to select it, drag to move it. Scroll to pan, pinch or ⌘-scroll to zoom. The footer shows the zoom, the preview quality and the size.
  4. Timeline. One row per layer, with time running left to right. The time display and play controls are along its top; the ruler below them is where you drag to move the playhead.
  5. Inspector (Properties). Every setting of the selected layer: Transform, Shape or Text, Masks and Effects. With nothing selected, it shows the composition's settings.
  6. Status bar. Live performance: frame time, GPU time and frame-cache use. Handy when playback slows down.
Need more room? ⌥⌘1–⌥⌘4 hide or show the Project panel, Timeline, Inspector and Status bar. ⌥⌘0 hides everything around the viewer and brings it back.
Lesson 1

Your first animation: a sliding ball

You'll make a circle glide from the left of the screen to the right in one second.

  1. On Home, click New Project (⌥⌘N).
  2. Add a circle: click the ellipse button in the toolbar, or choose Layer → New Ellipse (⌥⌘E). It appears in the middle of the viewer and as a row in the timeline.
  3. Drag the circle to the left side of the viewer.
  4. Press Home so the playhead is at the very start, 00:00:00.
  5. In the Inspector, find Transform → Position and click the stopwatch icon beside it. It turns gold, and a small diamond, your first keyframe, appears in the timeline.
  6. Move the playhead to one second: drag in the timeline ruler until the time display reads 00:01:00 (frame 30).
  7. Drag the circle to the right side of the viewer. Because the stopwatch is on, MotionForge writes a second keyframe for you.
  8. Press Space to play. The circle slides across. Press Space again to stop.

That is the whole idea of animation: turn on the stopwatch, move in time, change the value. Every lesson after this uses the same three moves.

Remember. Once a property's stopwatch is on, any change to it writes a keyframe at the playhead. If something jumps unexpectedly, check where the playhead was. ⌘Z undoes anything, and a whole drag is one undo step.
Making a reel? Press ⌘K for Composition Settings and pick the 9:16 preset (also 1:1, 4:5, 16:9 HD/4K, 21:9 or a custom size), the frame rate and the duration.
Lesson 2

Make it move naturally

Your circle moves at one steady speed and stops dead, like a machine. Real things speed up and slow down. That's easing.

Add an ease

  1. In the timeline, click the circle's first keyframe, then ⇧-click the second so both are selected. (You can also drag a box around them.)
  2. Right-click one of them and choose Easy Ease.
  3. Play again. The circle now starts gently and settles into place.

The same menu has Ease In (slow arrival), Ease Out (slow departure), Linear (constant speed) and Hold (no in-between: the value jumps when the next keyframe arrives).

Curve the path

When a layer with position keyframes is selected, its path is drawn as a dotted line in the viewer. Drag a square to move that keyframe, or a handle dot to bend the path into an arc. Hold ⌥ to move one handle on its own and make a sharp corner. Right-click a position keyframe to switch it between Smooth and Corner.

Fine-tune in the graph editor

Click Graph in the timeline's transport bar. The keyframe rows turn into curves of each value over time. Drag a handle sideways to stretch the ease, and up or down to change the speed. Pull a handle past the next value and the move overshoots and settles back, which gives a springy, bouncy feel. Click Graph again to go back.

Lesson 3

Fade, grow and spin

Position is one of five Transform properties every layer has:

  • Anchor Point: the point the layer scales and rotates around.
  • Position: where it is.
  • Scale: its size in percent. Width and height are linked; hold ⌘ to change one.
  • Rotation: its angle in degrees.
  • Opacity: 0% invisible, 100% solid.

Changing numbers quickly

Every number in the Inspector is hot text: drag left or right on it to scrub the value (hold ⇧ for big steps, ⌥ for fine ones), or click it and type. In the viewer, arrow keys nudge the selected layer 1 pixel, or 10 with ⇧.

Try it: a pop-in

  1. Add a star (toolbar or Layer → New Star) and press Home.
  2. Turn on the stopwatch for Scale and set it to 0%. Turn on Opacity and set it to 0%.
  3. Step forward 8 frames: press Page Down (or ⌘→) eight times. Set Scale to 115% and Opacity to 100%.
  4. Step forward 4 more frames and set Scale to 100%.
  5. Select all the keyframes, right-click, Easy Ease, and play. The star pops up, overshoots slightly and settles.
Show just what you need. Select a layer and press P Position, S Scale, R Rotation, T Opacity or A Anchor Point to show only that row in the timeline (⇧ adds another). U shows every animated property, UU every property you've changed. The ◀ ◆ ▶ buttons next to a property jump to the previous keyframe, add or remove one, and jump to the next.
Lesson 4

Animated text

Add text

  1. Click Aa in the toolbar, or choose Layer → New Text (⌥⇧⌘T).
  2. Type your words in the Inspector's Text box. Press Return for a new line.
  3. Choose a font from the menu under it and set left, centre or right alignment.
  4. Under Character, set Font Size, Tracking (space between letters), Line Height and Fill Colour. Each has a stopwatch, so they can be animated too.

The quick way: a text preset

Put the playhead where the text should start, open the Effects tab in the Project panel and double-click Typewriter, Fade Up by Character or Pop by Word under Text. Done.

How it works: text animators

Presets are built from text animators, which change some letters while leaving the rest alone. Here's a typewriter by hand:

  1. In Text Animators, click + and choose Opacity.
  2. Set its Char Opacity to 0%. Every letter inside the animator's range disappears, and right now the range is the whole text.
  3. Press Home, turn on the stopwatch for Range Start and leave it at 0.
  4. Move to 1.5 seconds and set Range Start to 100. As the range shrinks, the letters appear one by one.

Swap Opacity for a Position, Scale or Rotation animator, or switch the range from Characters to Words or Lines, and you have a different effect. The shape menu (Square, Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Triangle, Round, Smooth) softens the edge of the range.

Lesson 5

Presets and effects

Presets: animation in one double-click

Select one or more layers, put the playhead where the animation should begin, and double-click a preset in the Effects tab (or select it and click Apply):

  • Entrances: Fade In, Pop In, Slide Up, Spin In, Blur In
  • Exits: Fade Out, Slide Out Up
  • Attention: Glow Pulse, Bounce
  • Styles: Soft Shadow
  • Text: Typewriter, Fade Up by Character, Pop by Word

Presets just write ordinary keyframes and effects, so you can edit everything they add. Movement presets are relative: Slide Up ends exactly where your layer already is. Made something you like? Select the layer and choose Layer → Save Animation Preset…; it appears under My Presets.

Effects

Effects change how a layer looks. Add one from the Effects tab (search, then double-click), the Effect menu, or Add Effect in the Inspector:

  • Gaussian Blur: softens the layer.
  • Drop Shadow: colour, opacity, direction, distance and softness.
  • Glow: bright parts bleed light.
  • Brightness & Contrast, Hue/Saturation, Fill and Gradient Ramp for colour.

Each effect in the Inspector has a checkbox to switch it off for comparison, ▲▼ to change the order, and a stopwatch on every setting. Animate Blur Radius from 40 to 0 for a focus-in, or Glow Intensity up and down for a pulse.

Lesson 6

Shapes, masks and track mattes

Draw with the pen

  1. Press G (or click Pen in the toolbar).
  2. Click to place a sharp corner; click and drag to place a smooth point with handles.
  3. Click the first point to close the shape, or press Return to finish an open line.
  4. Press V or Esc to go back to the selection tool. Drag points and handles of the selected path to reshape it.

The switch next to the pen chooses what you draw: a New Shape (its own layer) or a Mask on Layer (cuts out part of the selected layer).

A line that draws itself

Turn on Stroke for your path and give it a width. In Shape Properties, keyframe Trim End from 0% to 100%: the line draws itself from start to finish. Great for underlines, arrows and signatures.

Masks

A mask shows only part of a layer. Click + in the Inspector's Masks section, or choose Layer → New Rectangle Mask / New Ellipse Mask. Set its mode (add, subtract, intersect, difference), feather (a soft edge), expansion and opacity, and invert it if you need the outside. All of them can be animated: an ellipse mask that grows from nothing is a classic reveal.

Track mattes

A track matte lets one layer decide where another is visible, for example a video showing only inside some big text. Select the layer to fill, open the Matte menu in the Inspector's Layer section, and pick the shaping layer and a mode: Alpha (visible where the matte is solid), Luma (visible where it's bright) or their inverted versions. The matte layer hides itself automatically.

Lesson 7

Footage, images and sound

Bring media in with ⌘I, the Import… button, or by dropping files onto the viewer or the Project panel.

  • Video: MOV, MP4, M4V, MPG, MTS, TS, DV and 3GP (H.264, HEVC, ProRes…). Clips play with their sound, and phone videos come in the right way up. MKV and WebM can't be read yet; convert them to MP4 first.
  • Images: PNG, JPEG, HEIC, WebP, AVIF, PSD, TIFF, OpenEXR, camera RAW and more, plus SVG and PDF artwork. PNGs with transparency stay transparent.
  • Audio: WAV, MP3, AAC/M4A, AIFF and CAF. Audio layers are teal in the timeline and show their waveform.

Drag a bar in the timeline to slide a clip in time; drag its ends to trim it. The speaker switch mutes an audio layer.

Fade music in and out

Audio layers have an Audio Level in decibels: 0 dB is as recorded, −48 dB is silent. Keyframe it from −48 to 0 over the first second for a fade-in, and back down at the end for a fade-out. Video clips have the same control for their sound.

Cut someone out of a video. Select a video or image layer and use Roto Brush in the Inspector. Choose Main Subject or People and MotionForge finds them in every frame of the work area and builds a mask that follows them. Put text behind the cut-out for a pro-looking depth effect.
Lesson 8

Keep bigger projects tidy

Name your layers

Select a layer and type a name at the top of the Inspector. “Title”, “Logo” and “BG” beat “Rectangle 4”.

Parenting: move things together

Set a layer's Parent (Inspector, Layer section) to another layer and it follows that layer's position, scale and rotation, while still keeping its own animation. A common trick is a null, an invisible layer that only exists to be a parent: Layer → New Null (⌥⇧⌘Y), parent a group of layers to it, then animate the null to move them all at once.

Pre-compose: a composition inside a composition

Select several layers and press ⇧⌘C. They're bundled into a new composition that sits in your main one as a single layer. Double-click it to open it; each open composition gets a tab above the timeline, and the trail under the tabs (main › scene › rig) takes you back up. ⇧⌘↑ goes to the parent.

Everyday editing

  • ⌘D duplicates, ⇧⌘D splits a layer at the playhead.
  • ] and [ move a layer forward or backward in the stack.
  • ⌘C / ⌘V copy and paste layers, or selected keyframes, even between projects. Pasted keyframes start at the playhead.
  • B and N set the start and end of the work area, the part of the timeline you're working on. Playback loops over it. Press 0 on the number pad for a RAM Preview: every frame is rendered once, then it plays in real time.
Lesson 9

A first look at 3D

  1. Make two or three layers (cards, text, images). Turn on the cube switch on each one's timeline row (or Layer → 3D Layer). They gain Z Position (depth) and X / Y Rotation.
  2. Give each a different Z Position, for example 0, 400 and 800. Positive Z pushes a layer further away.
  3. Add a camera: Layer → New Camera (⌥⇧⌘C). It starts out showing exactly what you saw before.
  4. Keyframe the camera's Position or Z Position over a few seconds and play. The near layers move faster than the far ones: that's parallax, and it's what makes 3D feel real.

To look around without changing your shot, pick Custom View in the viewer's footer, or press C for the Orbit Camera tool: drag to orbit, ⌥-drag to move in and out, ⇧-drag to pan. Exports always use your real camera.

Lesson 10

Export your video

Click Export in the toolbar, or choose File → Export Video… (⌘M).

FormatUse it for
H.264 (.mp4)Posting anywhere: Instagram, YouTube, WhatsApp. The safe choice.
HEVC (.mp4)The same quality in a smaller file, for newer devices.
ProRes 422 (.mov)Bringing your animation into DaVinci Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut at top quality.
ProRes 4444 (.mov)Overlays with a transparent background, such as titles or lower thirds to put over other footage.
PNG sequenceOne image per frame, also with transparency.
  • Size: full, half or quarter resolution.
  • Range: the whole composition or a custom part; it starts out as your work area if you set one.
  • Bitrate: Reels (10 Mb/s) for phone videos, YouTube (35 Mb/s) for long-form, or your own.
  • Audio: audio layers are mixed in at their levels, and you can add an extra soundtrack.
  • Motion blur: the viewer doesn't show it, but exports render it. Switch it on for smoother fast movement.

Exports run in the background from a snapshot of your project, so you can keep editing. Click Hide and a progress pill stays in the status bar; click it to bring the window back. Reveal shows the finished file in Finder.

Exporting needs MotionForge Pro. Editing, previewing and saving are free. Sign in with Google (toolbar, top right) and every new account gets a free Pro trial. Need a single still instead? File → Export Frame as PNG… (⇧⌘E) saves the frame at the playhead; turn on Composition → Transparency Grid first to keep the background transparent.
Bonus

Let Claude help

Claude can build and change animations in your open project for you. Describe what you want in plain words:

  • “Make a 5-second title card with my name that fades up.”
  • “Slow down the logo animation.”
  • “Add a glow to the selected layer.”

Every change appears live in the viewer and is one undo step, so you can try ideas safely. It's also a great way to learn: ask Claude to build something, then look at the keyframes and effects it made.

Open the panel with ✨ in the toolbar (⇧⌘L). There are two ways to connect:

  • Your Claude app: Connect Claude Desktop… or Copy Claude Code Command, then chat from there. This uses your Claude plan; no API key.
  • Right in the panel: paste an Anthropic API key. It's kept in your Mac's Keychain and usage is billed to that key.
Good habits

Saving and sharing

  • Save early: ⌘S saves a .mfproj file. Once it has a name, MotionForge auto-saves into it every 2 seconds (File → Auto Save Every 2 Seconds).
  • Crashes: untitled work is kept in a recovery copy, and MotionForge offers it back the next time it opens.
  • Moving to another Mac: File → Export Project Bundle… makes one .mfbundle file holding the project and copies of every image, clip and sound it uses. Double-click it on the other Mac to unpack and open it.
  • Editing together (Pro): Share → Start Sharing in the toolbar, then invite friends by the email they sign in with. Everyone's edits appear live, and each person's undo only undoes their own work.

Troubleshooting

When something goes wrong

macOS won't open the app (“Apple could not verify…”)

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to MotionForge. This is needed once, because the build isn't notarised by Apple yet.

Playback is choppy

Leave the preview quality in the viewer's footer on Auto: it drops to half or quarter resolution when a frame takes too long, and says so. You can also pick Half or Quarter yourself. For a perfectly smooth check, set a work area (B / N) and press 0 for a RAM Preview. Exports are always full quality.

A layer jumped, or keyframes appeared where I didn't want them

With a property's stopwatch on, every change writes a keyframe at the playhead. Check where the playhead is before you change a value, and press ⌘Z to undo.

I can't click the layer I want in the viewer

A big layer on top (a full-frame background or a layer with a track matte) can catch the clicks. Click the layer's name in the timeline instead, or hide the top layer with its eye icon while you work.

My video file won't import

MKV and WebM files can't be read yet. Convert them to MP4 or MOV and import again.

I can't see motion blur

The viewer shows frames without motion blur so it stays fast. Switch on Motion blur in the export window and it's rendered in the file.

Export opens a “Pro” window

Exporting needs MotionForge Pro. Sign in with Google to start the free trial, or choose a plan in that window. Creators can also ask about free access for a promotion reel: see Pricing.

The app closed unexpectedly

Open MotionForge again. Saved projects are auto-saved every 2 seconds, and untitled work is offered back from its recovery copy.

Keep going

Practice projects

The fastest way to get good is to finish small pieces. Try these in order; each uses the lessons above.

1 · Bouncing ball

A circle falls and bounces three times, each bounce lower. Position keyframes, Ease In on the way down, Ease Out on the way up, then shape it in the graph editor. Lessons 1–2

2 · Name lower third

A rounded rectangle slides in at the bottom left, your name types on over it, a subtitle fades up, and everything slides out after 4 seconds. Export it as ProRes 4444 to use over any video. Lessons 3–5, 10

3 · Logo reveal

Import a logo PNG. Reveal it with a growing ellipse mask, pop it in with a little overshoot, then add a Glow Pulse and a line drawn with Trim Paths underneath. Lessons 3, 5–7

4 · Reel title card

A 9:16 composition (⌘K) with a three-line title using Pop by Word, a music track that fades in and out, and a 3D camera drifting through two layers of shapes. Export with the Reels preset. Lessons 4, 7, 9, 10
